## Installation

```bash
npx shadcn@latest add https://liqui.design/r/tab-bar.json
```

## Usage

```tsx
import { TabBar, TabBarItem } from '@/components/ui/tab-bar';
```

```tsx
<TabBar value={tab} onValueChange={setTab}>
  <TabBarItem value="home" icon={<House fill="currentColor" strokeWidth={0} />}>
    Home
  </TabBarItem>
  <TabBarItem value="saved" icon={<Heart fill="currentColor" strokeWidth={0} />} badge={3}>
    Saved
  </TabBarItem>
</TabBar>
```

The bar is `position: static` and sizes to its items — put it wherever it
floats, usually pinned to the bottom of a scroll container with the content
padded to clear it.

## Notes

### The bar is the lens, not the pill

Apple's guidance is literal about this: a tab bar *floats above content at the
bottom of the screen*, and its items *rest on a Liquid Glass background that
allows content beneath to peek through*. So the bar refracts the page scrolling
underneath it, and the selection pill inside is paint.

It has to be. A `backdrop-filter` on the pill would sample what is painted
behind it, which inside the bar is the bar's own blurred and tinted output — a
lens on a lens, which arrives as a smudge. [Switch](/docs/components/switch)
documents the same trap from the other side.

[Tabs](/docs/components/tabs) resolves the question the opposite way, and the
contrast is the point:

| | Tabs | Tab Bar |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sits | *in* a page | *over* a page |
| Subject | the thing travelling between segments | the page showing through |
| Lens | the indicator | the bar |
| Indicator | glass | paint |

### Which buys the pill its stretch

Tabs has to force every segment to equal width. Its indicator *is* a lens, the
kernel keys a displacement map on size, and a pill that resizes mid-slide asks
for a new map on every frame of the transition — so it only interpolates
`translate`.

This pill is paint, so it can resize freely, and it does: it morphs between
labels of different lengths, and it stretches along its direction of travel in
proportion to how fast it is going. That stretch is tied to the spring's own
velocity rather than to a keyframe, so a hop between neighbours barely deforms
and a jump across the whole bar deforms a lot — which is what the eye expects of
something with mass.

### Press

Three things happen, and which ones depend on where your finger lands:

- The pressed item's **contents sink** to 0.9. The contents, not the button —
  scaling the button would move the box the pill measures itself against.
- On the tab you are **already on**, the pill squashes under the finger.
- On a tab you are **not** on, a highlight blooms under it instead. Stacking a
  second highlight on a box that already has the pill just looks like a bug.

### State comes from the DOM

The selected tab is read out of `data-active`, which Base UI writes, rather than
mirrored into React. The attribute is the truth, watching it costs one
`MutationObserver` instead of a context and a ref per item, and it keeps
`TabBarItem` a styled `Tabs.Tab` with nothing threaded through it.

A `ResizeObserver` runs the same measurement, because the bar is a row of
flexible items: a font swap or a container resize moves every box under the pill
without touching a single attribute.

### Following the guidance

- **Labels on every tab**, single words where possible. They are not optional
  here; a bar of bare icons is a guessing game.
- **Prefer filled symbols.** The demo passes `fill="currentColor"
  strokeWidth={0}` to Lucide icons to get there.
- **Don't disable or hide tabs.** A section with nothing in it should say so on
  its own screen; a bar whose items come and go reads as broken.
- **Badges are for information that warrants attention.** `badge={3}` draws a
  count, `badge` on its own draws a bare dot. A badge on everything is a badge
  on nothing.

  The drawn badge is `aria-hidden` and restated after the label, so the tab
  announces as "Saved 3 new items" rather than "3 Saved". There is no
  punctuation in that phrase on purpose — name computation joins the parts with
  a space of its own, and a leading comma arrives as "Saved , 3 new". Pass
  `badgeLabel` for wording of your own.
- **Keep the count low.** Five is comfortable; past that the labels start
  truncating and iOS would be collapsing the tail into a More tab.
