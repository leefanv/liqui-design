Most liqui components are a `LiquiGlass` surface with a component-shaped
content layer on top. Understanding the material is most of understanding the
library — and knowing [which components are deliberately not made of
it](#not-everything-is-glass) is the rest.

## The anatomy

A surface is four stacked layers plus your content:

| Layer | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Backdrop A** | `blur()` + `saturate()`. Never changes after first paint. |
| **Backdrop B** | Displacement only — `backdrop-filter: url(#…)`, sampling A's output. |
| **Tint** | A gradient carrying legibility, scaled by `frost`. |
| **Specular** | A canvas-rendered rim light that makes the edge read as polished. |
| **Content** | Your children, above all of it. |

Blur and displacement are on **separate layers** deliberately. While the filter's
`feImage` decodes its map, Chromium treats an entire
`url(#f) blur() saturate()` chain as inert — combined on one layer, even the
base blur would pop in late. Split, the surface is frosted from the first frame
and refraction fades in over it.

Those are the three Button variants on a flat fill. The two solid ones look
exactly as they do anywhere else. The glass one has gone slack — it is not
broken, there is simply nothing behind it to bend. Glass is a lens; a lens over
a solid colour shows you the solid colour.

Put surfaces over photography, video, a gradient with real edges, or content
that scrolls underneath. If your design calls for glass on a flat background,
you want `material="clear"` and a tint, not refraction.

## Not everything is glass

The line the library draws is the one Apple draws, and it is not about which
components are important enough to get the nice material:

> **Glass is for a surface you act *through*. A solid fill is for a control
> that carries a *value*.**

Buttons, popups, menus, toolbars, sheets and dialogs are the first kind — they
are the layer floating over your content, and letting that content through is
what the material is for. Checkbox, [Radio](/docs/components/radio-group),
[Toggle](/docs/components/toggle) and [Switch](/docs/components/switch) are the
second, and they are flat: `--lq-control` with a hairline at rest, `--lq-accent`
when they are on.

[Button](/docs/components/button) sits on both sides of the line, which is why
it is the clearest illustration of it. Its default variant is glass, because
pressing a button *does* something. Its `accent` and `danger` variants are
solid, because those say *this is the one* — and a tinted lens contradicts
itself: the colour is the whole message, and the lens exists to let the
wallpaper through it. Accent over a night photograph and accent over a bright
one were two different colours.

This is also why a brand colour never needs `frost` tuning in liqui. Nothing
carrying `--lq-accent` is a backdrop-filtered surface, so there is no
transparency for a backdrop to leak through in the first place.

## The dials

| Prop | Range | What it controls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `frost` | 0–1 | Material density. `0` ≈ Apple's *clear* (needs a busy backdrop and its own dimming), `1` ≈ *regular* (adaptive frosted, safe anywhere). Default `0.35`. |
| `refraction` | px | `feDisplacementMap` scale — how hard the rim bends. |
| `bezel` | px | How wide the refracting rim is. Menus want ~28, buttons ~11. |
| `blur` | px | Base blur. `frost` adds up to 14px on top. |
| `dispersion` | 0+ | Chromatic aberration. Costs ~3× filter work — leave at `0` unless it's a hero surface. |
| `specular` | 0–1 | Rim light opacity. |
| `radius` | px | Corner radius. Also drives the displacement map's shape. |
| `material` | `auto` \| `frost` \| `clear` | Rendering tier. `auto` refracts where supported and frosts elsewhere. |
| `finish` | `classic` \| `prism` | How the surface is lit. `classic` lights the top and bottom edges and frosts the pane. `prism` is one key light from the top left, a dark far rim, a hairline round the outline and a clear, brightened interior — glass type, as a surface. Wants a narrow bezel, strong refraction and a little dispersion; `prismGlassTheme` is that tuning. |
| `lightAngle` | ° | Where the light comes from, clockwise from above. Unset, the finish's own: `0` for classic, `-33` for prism. |
| `edgeShade`, `hairline`, `brightness`, `tintFloor` | 0–1 | The rest of the lighting: shade on the edges facing away from the light, opacity of the outline, brightness pushed through the glass, and the tint left at `frost: 0`. Each follows the finish unless set. |

Each of these is a per-surface prop, and the components set the geometric ones
(`radius`, `refraction`, `bezel`) to values scaled to their own size. To move
them all at once — or to change the colours — see [Theming](/docs/handbook/theming).

## Corners

Every corner in liqui is a continuous one — the squircle Apple draws, where the
curve eases out of the straight edge instead of meeting it at a seam — and every
component draws it the same way.

**A glass surface** gets it from the kernel. `radius` is the round-corner size
you mean; where `corner-shape: squircle` renders (Chromium 139+), the kernel
raises it 1.4× so the softer curve reads the same size, and draws the map and
the lighting on that curve. A surface whose corners reach half its height is a
capsule or a disc, and keeps a true semicircle — a superellipse cannot close
into one.

**Anything inside a surface** — a hover wash, a highlighted menu item, a
pressed toggle — follows the surface. The surface publishes its corner as
`--lq-radius` and its shape as `--lq-corner`, and a container states its inset
once, next to its padding, with the height of what it holds:

```tsx
// The container: its padding, and its items' height.
<div className="p-1.5 lq-nest [--lq-inset:6px] [--lq-item-h:24px]">
  {/* The item: the surface's radius less the inset, on the surface's shape. */}
  <div className="lq-corner rounded-[var(--lq-item-radius)]" />
</div>
```

Same centre, parallel curves, an even gap all the way round — and a theme's
`radiusScale` reaches the items through the surface, so they never drift out of
step with it. Where the concentric radius comes out at half an item's height or
more, the item is a capsule, and `lq-nest` gives it half its height on round
corners — the rule the kernel applies to a surface. On a capsule strip the
items come out capsules too.

**Anything with a radius of its own** — a solid button, a checkbox, a chip —
takes `lq-squircle`, with its radius as written and its height:

```tsx
<span className="lq-squircle [--lq-r:5px] [--lq-h:16px]" />
```

It gets exactly what a glass surface gets from the kernel: the radius 1.4×
larger where the squircle renders, times the theme's `radiusScale`, and a true
capsule once that reaches half the height. A box *around* a glass surface —
the groove a tab indicator slides in — is the surface's corner plus the inset.
Circles and pills stay `rounded-full`.

Safari and Firefox do not draw `corner-shape` yet. There every corner is round
at the radius as written — the same look liqui had before the squircle.

## Performance budget

The expensive parts are the canvas-generated displacement map and the
`backdrop-filter` chain, in that order.

- **Maps are cached** module-wide, keyed by size + optics. Ten buttons of the
  same size cost one map.
- **Filters are hoisted** into a single persistent hidden `<svg>` registry, so a
  remounted surface references an already-decoded filter and refracts on the
  first frame. This is why reopening a menu has no warm-up.
- **Generation is half-resolution** above roughly 180×180.
- `dispersion > 0` triples the filter work. Reserve it.
- Stacking many large `backdrop-filter` surfaces is the one thing that will
  actually cost you frames. Prefer one large surface over a dozen small ones.

## Degradation

| Browser | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| **Chromium** | Full refraction. |
| **Safari** | Drops `backdrop-filter` entirely when it references an SVG filter. Falls back to frosted blur automatically. WebKit bug 245510 has an implementation in review. |
| **Firefox** | Same fallback. |

The fallback is automatic — `material="auto"` detects support at module load.
There is nothing to configure, but there *is* something to check: **look at your
UI in Safari.** A design tuned to `frost: 0` can be unreadable once refraction
is gone, because in the refraction tier the lens was doing the work the tint
would otherwise have to do.

Also honour user preference — a surface that ignores
`prefers-reduced-transparency` is an accessibility bug, not a style choice:

```css
@media (prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce) {
  .liqui-glass {
    --lq-tint: var(--fd-background);
    --lq-tint-deep: var(--fd-background);
  }
}
```

## Two things that will bite you

**Never measure a surface with `getBoundingClientRect`.** Popups open under a
`scale()` transition, and a rect measured mid-animation bakes a permanently
undersized displacement map that nothing re-fires to correct. The result looks
like weak frosted blur and is very hard to trace back. liqui uses
`ResizeObserver` `contentRect` and `offsetWidth`, both of which are layout
sizes and immune to transforms.

**Never build the displacement map as an SVG data URI.** Chromium rasterizes
`feImage` SVG sources with CSS features disabled — `mix-blend-mode` and modern
`hsl()` silently do nothing, corrupting the map into a full-surface smear.
Generate it on a canvas.
